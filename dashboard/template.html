Glossary

Tj
Junction temperature: the hottest point on the die. Most of it here comes from the heat sink and TIM-2, not TIM-1, so designs are ranked by θJC instead.
Tcase
Case temperature: the lid top at its centre, above the die.
θJC
Junction-to-case thermal resistance, (Tj − Tcase) / 400 W, in °C/W. Lower is better cooling. It does not depend on how good the heat sink is.
TIM-1, TIM-2
Thermal interface materials. TIM-1 sits between the die and the lid, TIM-2 between the lid and the heat sink.
k
Thermal conductivity, W/mK. Higher moves heat better.
E
Young's modulus, MPa. Higher is stiffer, so the TIM resists the die and lid expanding differently and carries more stress.
CTE
Coefficient of thermal expansion, ppm/K: how much the material grows when heated.
BLT
Bond line thickness, µm: how thick the TIM layer is.
Edge shear
Maximum shear stress on a 1 mm path 0.5 to 1.5 mm in from the TIM corner, at the TIM mid-plane. Starting 0.5 mm in avoids the corner singularity, so the value does not grow with mesh refinement.
Warpage
Out-of-plane bow of the substrate, max minus min vertical displacement, µm.
Pareto set
The designs that no other design beats on both cooling and stress at the same time. Choosing among them is a trade-off.
Surrogate
A Gaussian process fitted to the Ansys runs. It predicts a new design in milliseconds instead of about 3.5 minutes.
Hold-out runs
Runs kept out of training and used only to measure the surrogate's error.
Indium stress
Indium is modelled as linear elastic. Real indium yields at a few MPa and creeps, so its edge stress here is an upper bound, not a prediction.
